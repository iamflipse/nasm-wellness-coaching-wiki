# Social Wellness, Social Capacity and Social Media

*Lessons covered: 29 (Social Media), 30 (Social Wellness), 31 (Social Capacity)*

## Social connection is a biological need (Lesson 30)

- Humans are exceedingly social. Social relationships are described as **as important to health as eating right, sleeping well and not being stressed**.
- Humans have special brain cells, **von Economo neurons**, linked to regulating emotion in social contexts. They are found only in highly intelligent, social animals and may have evolved with social behaviour.

### The loneliness problem (figures as stated in the course)

| Statistic | Figure |
|---|---|
| US adults who feel lonely pretty much all the time | About **36%** |
| Young adults (about 18–25) reporting serious, frequent loneliness | **61%** |
| Reduction in early-death risk with healthy social relationships | About **50%** |

- Loneliness is linked with many negative health outcomes. The course says it is **worse for health than obesity** and **as bad as smoking**.
- There is a **dose–response relationship**: even small improvements in social connection can improve overall health.

### Quality over quantity

- It's the **quality** of relationships, not the number of friends, that matters.
- Both **giving and receiving** social support count.
- **Dunbar's number** is a suggested cognitive limit on how many stable relationships we can maintain. Beyond it, relationships tend to be superficial and unstable.
- Suggest **active community groups**: being part of a group with shared interests is described as excellent for social wellness.

### The 200,000-year-old brain

We have a natural **negativity bias** about other people, and the brain even rewards negative thinking with **dopamine**. For early humans it was safer to assume a rustling bush was a tiger, not a bunny. Now that ancient brain faces complicated modern social situations. Coaches can ask: *are we overthinking, or thinking too negatively, about this social situation?*

## Social media (Lessons 29–30)

| Less helpful | More helpful |
|---|---|
| **Passive scrolling** and consuming others' posts | **Meaningful, supportive interactions**: commenting, liking, amplifying others |
| Letting it "wash over you", which leads to **social comparison** and **self-judgement** | Showing something interesting to the person **next to you**, offline |
| Heavy use **and** strong emotional attachment to it (linked with poorer mental health, depression and anxiety) | Sharing thoughtfully, as **an offering that reflects your values** |
| Treating it as a replacement for real relationships | Using it to **nourish real-life relationships** |

Social media scratches the itch for connection, but it **can't replace real-life relationships** or real-life social needs. Sometimes it helps to put the visual stimulation aside and experience life **from the inside out**.

## Social capacity and social capital (Lesson 31)

- Relationships are a source of support and fun, but **too much of a good thing** is possible. Coaches help clients navigate their **social capacity**, starting by reflecting on their own limits.
- **Social capital**: quality relationships built on **trust and belonging**, plus the networks and communities that meet the need for participation and engagement.
- People empathise and bond more easily with their **in-group**, those who share values and norms.
- Identify relationships that add **unnecessary stress** or lack **reciprocity**. Consider mending them or **setting boundaries**.

## Coaching in practice

- "Who are the people who leave you feeling better after you've seen them?"
- "When did you last have a real conversation with someone you care about?"
- "Is there a group or community, a class, a club, a volunteer role, that matches something you love?"
- "How do you feel after scrolling? What would make your time online more connecting?"
- "Are there relationships that are draining you? What boundary might help?"
- "Could you be overthinking what they meant? What's another explanation?"
