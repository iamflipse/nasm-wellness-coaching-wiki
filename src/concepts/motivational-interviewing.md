# Motivational Interviewing and the Coaching Continuum

*Lessons covered: 10 (Motivational Interviewing), 56 (Motivational Interviewing), 60 (Coaching Along the Continuum), 61 (Coaching Continuum Q&A)*

## What motivational interviewing is

- **Motivational interviewing (MI)** is a **client-centred communication style** for understanding a client's priorities and building an action plan. It is especially useful with clients who are **ambivalent** about change (Lesson 10).
- Its fundamentals: **ask the right questions, listen actively and understand the reply**, then respond with constructive information or advice (Lesson 10).
- It **targets resistance to change** and lets change feel **organic and collaborative**.
- MI is linked to **self-determination theory**, the idea that humans inherently seek growth. Supporting **autonomy** and a feeling of **competence** can be a powerful catalyst for **self-efficacy** (Lesson 10).
- It starts with the client feeling **accepted and heard without judgement**, and without being handed advice straight away. Trust comes before feedback (Lesson 56).
- Being heard opens the client up to talk about what they really want to change and why, which leads to **commitment** to the eventual plan (Lesson 56).

## OARS

| Letter | Tool | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **O** | **Open-ended questions** | Learn about the client's experiences, thoughts and beliefs | "What would you like to be different?" |
| **A** | **Affirmations** | Help clients notice their strengths, values and past successes to build confidence. Most people dismiss strengths and fixate on weaknesses; affirmations flip that. They should be grounded in truth and based on behaviour | "There is no wrong decision here." "Remember that you're not your mistakes." |
| **R** | **Reflective listening** | Reflect back what was said in the moment; shows interest and builds trust | "It sounds like evenings are the hardest part." |
| **S** | **Summarising** | Pull the whole conversation together; check how the client feels or move to a new topic. Often triggers moments of self-realisation | See the Lesson 56 example below |

**Summary example (paraphrasing Lesson 56):** the coach sums up that the client is ready for change and wants to start by adding one new vegetable a day and drinking 8 more ounces of water daily, calls it a wonderful start, and agrees to check in next week.

**Reflection vs summary:** a reflection responds to one statement in the moment; a summary covers the whole conversation and gives back the full picture.

### Sharing expertise

Share what you know in a **neutral, non-judgemental** way, then **ask for the client's thoughts** on it. The client still guides the conversation. Clients should be allowed to **make mistakes**, learn from them and be motivated to try again. The aim of every session is to **enhance autonomy** through collaborative goal setting and action planning.

## Change talk: DARN and CAT (Lesson 60)

**Change talk** is language showing the client's motivation to change. Asking questions that **evoke** it helps move conversations forward.

| Acronym | Stands for | Example question |
|---|---|---|
| **D**ARN | **Desire** | "What would you like to be different?" |
| D**A**RN | **Ability** | "What do you think you could manage?" |
| DA**R**N | **Reasons** | "What are your reasons for wanting this?" |
| DAR**N** | **Need** | "How important is this to you right now?" |
| **C**AT | **Commitment** | "What are you willing to commit to?" |
| C**A**T | **Activation** | "What are you ready to do this week?" |
| CA**T** | **Taking steps** | "What steps have you already taken?" |

The example questions are illustrations, not quotations from the course.

## Coaching along the continuum (Lesson 60)

Even the best plans go off course. Life happens: **acknowledge it, normalise it and keep moving forward.** The bigger goal is for clients to recognise the **levels of self-efficacy** they move through and overcome new obstacles using what they learned in coaching.

| | Clients with lower self-efficacy | Clients with higher self-efficacy |
|---|---|---|
| **Presentation** | Self-doubt, stepping back, can't build momentum, too hard on themselves | Strong self-belief, intrinsic motivation, willing to take more risk |
| **Coach focus** | **Compassion and empathy**; validate struggles and wins; **revisit the wellness vision**; use the core communication tools (OARS) and change-talk questions (DARN, CAT) | **Expand the growth mindset**; support experiments with integrated feedback; sometimes remind them to **stay realistic** |
| **Tools** | Paraphrase and clarify; ask questions that help them expand on feelings; let them **process independently**; recall past successes; brainstorm steps and possible setbacks | Regular **check-ins** to keep alignment with the vision |

**For everyone:**

- When setting new goals, **leave room for experimentation, setbacks and risk**. Present setbacks as **information** about what does and doesn't work.
- **Build new routines** together, including daily wellness practices.
- Resilience rests on **self-awareness and self-acceptance**.
- **Always recognise success**, however small. It keeps momentum and self-belief going.

### Finding the real motivation (Lesson 61)

With an unmotivated or ambivalent client, it often means **you haven't yet hit the right nerve**. It is the coach's job to dig for the **kernel of motivation** that strikes a chord. Your reasons for their change may differ from **their core motivation**.

## Coaching in practice

- "What would you like to talk about today?"
- "On a scale of 1 to 10, how important is this to you? Why not lower?"
- "You stuck with it through a really busy week. That says something about your determination." (affirmation)
- "So what I'm hearing is…" (reflection)
- "Let me sum up what we've covered…" (summary)
- "Tell me about a time you faced a challenge like this and got through it."
- "What's the real reason this matters to *you*?"
