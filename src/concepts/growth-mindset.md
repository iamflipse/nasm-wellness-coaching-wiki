# Growth Mindset

*Lessons covered: 2 (Growth Mindset); also draws on 24 (Performance & Cognition) and 60 (Coaching Along the Continuum)*

## Key ideas

- A **growth mindset** is the belief that abilities can grow with effort, learning and practice. Lesson 60 puts it as the idea that intelligence can be nurtured with learning and effort.
- A **fixed mindset** tends to **resist** learning opportunities. A growth mindset tends to **embrace** them (Lesson 24).
- **Mindsets are not static.** Coaches can help clients move from fixed to growth through persistence, effort and practice. A growth mindset improves attitudes to learning, builds resilience and can improve personal outcomes (Lesson 24).
- Growth mindset is part of **cognitive health**. It shows up in self-attributions such as "I can do this", "I'm working hard", "I'm making progress" (Lesson 24).
- Clients with **high self-efficacy** usually already have a growth mindset and intrinsic motivation. Coaching them is about expanding that mindset. Sometimes it also means reminding them to stay realistic (Lesson 60).

## How to cultivate a growth mindset (Lesson 2)

| Strategy | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Look for inspiration** | Seeing what is possible for others reminds you what is possible for you |
| **Set meaningful goals** | Science suggests growth mindset helps most when goals matter to the person |
| **Seek support** | Find instructors, coaches, mentors and peers who share your goals and can give information and feedback |
| **Celebrate and document progress** | Recording progress and sharing it with others creates an upward spiral of confidence for the next goal |
| **Look backward** | Recall things you learned or did that weren't easy. Past growth gives confidence about future growth |

The lesson stresses that growth is **not a do-it-yourself project**. Building community and support is part of the plan.

## Movement as growth-mindset training

Lesson 53 adds that **movement milestones** are a direct way to train a growth mindset: you take on a challenge, ask for and accept support, put in effort, make progress and finally master something. That concrete evidence can carry over to other life goals. See [Movement Motivation](movement-motivation.md).

## Coaching in practice

- "Think back to something you learned that wasn't easy at first. How did you get through it?"
- "Who could support you with this goal: a friend, a class, a group?"
- "How would you like to keep track of your progress so you can see it?"
- "What would it look like to treat this as something you're *learning*, not something you're either good or bad at?"
- With a high-efficacy client: "I love your ambition. What's a realistic first version of this?"
