# Upward Spirals and the Transfer Effect

*Lessons covered: 58 (Upward Spirals), 59 (Upward Spirals Q&A), 18 (Incorporating Movement: purpose and activity)*

## Key ideas

- An **upward spiral** is a psychological phenomenon in which **one positive behaviour makes another more likely**, creating a **positive feedback loop**.
- Example from the lesson: a good night's sleep → you feel better → you're more likely to eat well → you have energy to be active → you probably sleep better. All of it lifts mood; sustained long enough, it lifts **disposition**.
- The theory is based on research by **Barbara Fredrickson** and her team.

## Why positive emotions matter: broadening

Negative emotions trigger **specific, narrow impulses**. Positive emotions **broaden** thinking, making people more mentally flexible, creative, cooperative and open to new experiences.

| Emotion | Impulse it triggers |
|---|---|
| Anger | Lash out |
| Fear | Flee |
| Joy | Playfulness, exploration |
| Love | Curiosity, vulnerability, connection |
| Gratitude | Endearment, reciprocity |
| Hope | Problem-solving, tenacity |
| Interest, amusement | Creativity |

Even when someone doesn't act on a negative impulse, brain imaging shows the impulse is still there. Positive emotion brings **neurological flexibility**, which makes lasting change possible. The lesson's point: specific behaviours matter, but it is **the positive emotions that broaden and change the brain**.

## Documented upward spirals (as listed in Lesson 58)

- Self-concordant goals ↔ reaching goals ↔ happiness
- Positive emotions ↔ vagal tone
- Loving-kindness meditation ↔ mood
- Gratitude practice ↔ humility
- Positive connections with others ↔ positive disposition and meaning
- Going for walks ↔ good mood
- Healthy behaviours (exercise, eating well) ↔ good mood
- **Sense of purpose ↔ physical activity**

### Purpose and physical activity (Lesson 18)

A study of about **14,000 Americans** measured physical activity and sense of purpose, then checked again four years later. Regularly active people showed a **greater increase in purpose**, and people with a sense of purpose at the start were **more likely to exercise regularly**. The relationship runs both ways: taking care of yourself helps you find a reason to, and having a big why helps you take care of yourself.

## The transfer effect

When someone makes a positive change in one area, it **spreads into others**. The skills of change and the gains in resilience give a boost elsewhere. Examples from the course:

- Better sleep → better mood → kinder to people → better relationships
- Focusing on sleep → eating better
- Eating right → working out harder
- Working on mindfulness and mindset → doubling down on sleep

## Incremental change (Lesson 59)

Most people need **small changes linked together**. One small success leads to a second, third and fourth, and over time these add up to big change. Examples the expert gives:

- Drink more water each day
- Deep breathing for 3 minutes, twice a day, to reduce stress
- Pick two vegetables a day

The coach **reinforces** each small change so it becomes a sustained behaviour, and the lifestyle the client came for follows.

## Coaching in practice

- "Since you started sleeping better, have you noticed anything else changing?"
- "What's one small win from this week we can build on?"
- "Which change feels like it would make the others easier?"
- "How did you feel after that walk?" (savouring the positive emotion strengthens the loop)
- "What gives you a sense of purpose? How might moving more connect to that?"
