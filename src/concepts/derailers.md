# Situations That Derail

*Lesson covered: 73 (Situations that Derail)*

## Key ideas

- Not every session goes smoothly. **Change is hard**, and people react differently to being pushed out of their comfort zone.
- Most clients are glad to take part, but some behaviours **challenge the coaching relationship**.
- These are **normal**. Put them in context and appreciate what the client may be going through.
- A person's actions **almost always reflect an underlying need or struggle**. When people can't voice a need or inner conflict, it often comes out as **discordant behaviour**. When something isn't working, people fall back on **familiar, comfortable behaviour**.

## Common derailers

| Behaviour | What may be underneath |
|---|---|
| Trouble with **follow-through** | Overwhelm; a plan that isn't realistic yet |
| **Hesitation** to try new things | Fear, discomfort with change |
| **Stuck**: repeating unproductive behaviours despite agreeing to change | Falling back on the familiar when something isn't working |
| **Low motivation or self-efficacy** | Not yet connected to their real why |
| Behaviour that feels **unreceptive or disrespectful** | Feeling challenged or anxious because of internal or external factors; changes in environment or support |

## How to respond

1. **Stay curious, empathetic and kind.** Use motivational interviewing and reflective listening.
2. **Stay within your scope of practice.** Open communication makes it easier to set and keep **boundaries**.
3. **Name what you notice, neutrally.** Use statements that leave room for the client's own perspective. Feeling heard helps turn things around.
4. **Use reflective listening**, including **amplified reflection** (below).
5. **Take it in without taking it on.** Acknowledge their feelings and clarify what's behind them **without getting defensive**. It is probably **not about you**; it may be how they're processing change.
6. **Validate** emotions about what's changing in their world, then explore ways to address their concerns and **re-engage** together.
7. **Use feedback to adjust.** Their reaction may show you how to meet them where they are and sharpen your skills.

### Amplified reflection

When a client makes a strong **all-or-nothing** statement under pressure ("This is the worst thing that could happen"), repeat it back in an **exaggerated** form ("Yes, this is the absolute worst thing that could ever happen"). Hearing it amplified can help them **rethink how true it is**.

### Prevent feedback friction

Feedback can feel critical depending on how either side delivers it. **In the first session, agree how you each prefer to give and receive feedback.** Keep paying attention to your language as challenges come up.

**Aim of your reflections:** encourage **clarification, exploration and self-empathy**, and open space to brainstorm **re-engagement** and **renewed motivation**.

Keep **realistic expectations** about what may come up in a coaching career, and be confident you have the tools to handle it.

## Coaching in practice

- "I've noticed the walks haven't happened the last couple of weeks. What's been going on?"
- "It sounds like a lot has changed at home. How are you doing with all of that?"
- "Nothing at all has worked, ever?" (amplified reflection, said gently)
- "What would make this feel more doable right now?"
- "How is our way of working together landing for you? Is there something I could do differently?"
- "Last time we agreed you'd tell me if feedback felt too blunt. How did today feel?"
