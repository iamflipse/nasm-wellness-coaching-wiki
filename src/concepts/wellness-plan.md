# Creating, Monitoring and Changing the Wellness Plan

*Lessons covered: 69 (Creating a Wellness Plan), 71 (Monitoring and Its Importance), 72 (Changing the Wellness Plan); vision questions from 70 (Wellness Mindset)*

## Creating the plan (Lesson 69)

- The client comes to you, but **the client directs the process**. The plan is **client-directed and collaborative**, built around their desires and **value-centred goals**.
- If a client says "just tell me what to do", remind them that your role is to help them tap into **their own strength and self-awareness**. They know themselves best. Redirect them to their **wellness vision**, their intake **assessments and forms**, and use **motivational interviewing**.
- **Realistic goals they are confident they can reach** keep building self-efficacy. Every win counts, and it counts more when the idea comes from the client.
- The most important input is the **scope of the wellness vision**. The plan must **line up with the vision**, so every step visibly moves them closer. The vision will change over time, so stay open.
- **Identify the support system:** family and friends help commitment outside sessions.

### Plan document structure

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Top** | The client's **wellness vision**, in their words |
| **Short-term goals** | Near-term goals that serve the vision |
| **Action steps** | Small steps the client can confidently carry out **between sessions** |
| **Supports** | Who and what helps (or hinders) |

It can be on paper or digital.

### Session rhythm

- Sessions are typically **once a week**. That leaves time to put commitments into practice, see how support systems help or hinder, and discover strengths.
- At the next session: share how it went, **celebrate wins**, and decide what to explore further.

### Vision questions (Lesson 70)

"What brought you here?" · "What is your definition of wellness?" · "What would be different in your life?" · "What's holding you back?" · "What would wellness be in service of?" See [Get To vs Have To](get-to-vs-have-to.md) for the full list.

## Monitoring (Lesson 71)

**Monitoring** is the systematic, continuous collection of observations and data: tracking the client's experiences while **holding them capable and able**.

**What regular check-ins do:**

- Give time to reflect on **what's working** and **celebrate victories**. Recognising achievements gives **self-efficacy an automatic boost**.
- Identify **what made progress happen**.
- Discuss **what's been challenging**, and adjust the process, goals or action steps.
- Review the client's **self-awareness** of their tendencies, motivations and understanding, not just your observations.
- Spot **shifts in what's truly meaningful** to the client.

**Tools:** motivational interviewing and open-ended questions; **assessment tools** on self-reflection and mindfulness; **questionnaires** on specific wellness areas.

**Why it matters:** people often "can't see the forest for the trees". An outside view and **objective encouragement** help them see how far they've come.

## Changing the plan (Lesson 72)

- **The only constant is change.** In coaching, change is **normal, expected and a sign of growth**.
- Example from the lesson: a client starts out wanting to lose weight, but over time it emerges that the real goal is **better balance** between work, sleep and overall health.

**How to change the plan well:**

1. **Discuss together** how the current plan will be affected before starting a new path.
2. **Build on what's already in motion.** What has the client learned that carries over? Which **strengths** will help with new goals?
3. **Look at earlier assessments** for ideas.
4. Keep every change **client-centred and co-created**.
5. **Rebalance goals** so focus doesn't lean too heavily on one goal or start too long-term. Lead with **small victories**, especially when short-term goals need intense focus.
6. **Normalise and celebrate change.** Treat it as a win and a sign of greater possibility, not a failure.

## Coaching in practice

- "Let's write your vision at the top, in your own words."
- "What's one step you're confident you can take before we meet next week?" Then: "How confident, 1 to 10?"
- "Who in your life could support this?"
- "What went well this week? What made that possible?"
- "What was hardest? What might we adjust?"
- "Looking back to when we started, what's different now?"
- "It sounds like what matters to you has shifted. Shall we revisit the plan together?"
