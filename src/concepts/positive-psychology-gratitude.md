# Positive Psychology and Gratitude

*Lessons covered: 19 (Positive Psychology), 20 (Positive Psychology Q&A), 33 (Gratitude)*

## Positive psychology (Lesson 19)

- **Positive psychology** is the scientific study of human wellbeing.
- Traditional psychology studied dysfunction: mental illness and disorders such as anxiety, depression and schizophrenia. The founders of positive psychology noticed that **the absence of disease is not the same as the presence of wellbeing**, and asked what makes a fulfilling life.
- Researchers studied people with **high life satisfaction**, drawing on everything from ancient philosophy to modern self-help, to find what mattered to people who were doing well.

### PERMA (+V)

The best-known model, from psychologist **Martin Seligman**:

| Letter | Element |
|---|---|
| **P** | Positive emotion |
| **E** | Engagement (including flow) |
| **R** | Relationships |
| **M** | Meaning |
| **A** | Accomplishment |
| **+V** | **Vitality**, which the course adds |

Adding **vitality** ties wellness coaching to positive psychology: body and mind are **one integrated whole**, and physical activity, sleep and nutrition affect mood, productivity and clarity of thought.

### Research questions and tools

The field asks how to spark positive emotion, increase engagement and flow, build strong relationships and communicate well, make people more sensitive to positive experiences, find meaning at work, understand self-transcendence and cross-cultural differences, and scale wellbeing up.

Tools named in the course: **focusing on strengths**, **gratitude practice** to counter negativity bias, **mattering to others**, and **cultivating resilience**.

## Toxic positivity vs pragmatic optimism (Lesson 20)

**Learning resources** the expert recommends: Laurie Santos's Coursera course from Yale; the Applied Positive Psychology programme at the University of Pennsylvania; Martin Seligman's book *Flourish*.

| | Toxic positivity | Pragmatic optimism |
|---|---|---|
| **Stance** | Denies legitimate suffering; "just buck up and focus on the good" | Honest about the situation, the resources and what is really happening, including when it's genuinely bad |
| **Result** | Negative emotions get stuffed down and come back up; no healing | Honesty makes it possible to choose a proactive, hopeful next step |
| **Coach's role** | The opposite of a wellness coach's role | Recognise the person; meet basic human needs first if they're struggling |

Positivity becomes toxic when the focus on being perfect becomes painful and leaves no room to breathe. **Humility, honesty and vulnerability** about our struggles protect against it.

## Gratitude (Lesson 33)

- All positive emotions increase **neurological flexibility**, and gratitude is no exception.
- Gratitude makes us more **prosocial** (connected to others, wanting to do good), increases **humility**, and opens us to appreciating people and positive experiences.
- **Daily gratitude practice** is practice in feeling positive emotion and being open and connected. Connection is linked with greater wellbeing and life satisfaction.
- Healthy behaviours that connect us to others are **more likely to be repeated**.
- Practising gratitude means practising the neurological state of **connection, vulnerability, humility and openness**, all part of building lasting change and long-term resilience.

**Gratitude and the brain (Lesson 22):** gratitude has large benefits for cognition and for handling daily emotional hassles. Research on compassion meditation and gratitude suggests that with practice, brain areas associated with being **happy at rest** become more active, in trained regular people too. (See [Stress and Recovery](stress-and-recovery.md).)

## Coaching in practice

- "What's going well for you right now, even something small?"
- "What strengths have you relied on lately?"
- "That sounds really hard. Let's be honest about where things are. What's one hopeful step from here?"
- "Would you be open to trying a quick gratitude practice, like noting three things each evening?"
- "Who in your life makes you feel like you matter? Who do you matter to?"
- Avoid: "Just look on the bright side!"
