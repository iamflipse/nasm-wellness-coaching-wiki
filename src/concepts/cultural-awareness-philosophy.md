# Cultural Awareness and Coaching Philosophy

*Lessons covered: 62 (Cultural Awareness), 63 (Coaching Philosophy), 64 (Creating a Coaching Philosophy 1), 65 (Creating a Coaching Philosophy 2: no transcript available)*

Both topics start from the same place: **knowing your own baseline** (your background, identity, culture, values and relationship to wellness) so you can show up well for clients.

## Cultural awareness (Lesson 62)

- Everyone brings unique **life experiences, identities and values** into sessions. Coaching happens inside a **larger cultural context** that shapes each person's relationship to wellness.
- Recognising this in clients **and in yourself** helps you coach with curiosity, **release your ego** and centre the client. The aim is not to deny your own perceptions but to **honour differences**.
- **Culturally responsive coaching:** clients feel safe bringing **the whole of their identities and experiences** and know they'll be received with care. You don't have to agree with or even understand them, but you must coach with **curiosity and respect**.

### Establishing your baseline

Your professional background, life experiences, identity, culture and relationship to wellness all shape how you show up. Knowing **what is true and normal for you** helps you notice your own assumptions and projections, and move from **default mode** into **reflective curiosity**.

**Identity markers** place a person closer to or further from **power and privilege**. This is constructed at the societal level, and both coach and client likely hold a mix of **dominant and non-dominant** identities.

### Bias

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Implicit (unconscious) bias** | Stereotypes or judgements you hold without awareness that shape your responses |
| **Explicit (conscious) bias** | Judgements you hold with deliberate awareness |

Everyone has both. Unchecked, they keep you in unhelpful patterns or cause **unintentional harm**. When it happens: **apologise and repair**, and offer yourself **compassion** without self-judgement. Make room for genuine inquiry **without projection**.

### Emotional triggers

Past experiences and implicit bias can trigger a **fight-or-flight** reaction in session. To come back to the present: **deep breathing**, feeling your **feet on the ground**, a **hand on your belly or heart**, or quiet self-talk. Keep the focus on the client so you **respond rather than react**.

### Equity and communication culture

- **Equity awareness:** people don't start from the same place. Environmental and social factors shape everyone's relationship to wellness.
- **Coaching communication culture:** the intentional way you relate to each client. It combines your **natural, authentic style**, your **training**, and the **client's communication needs and preferences**, using active and reflective listening, skilful questions and thoughtful responses.

## Coaching philosophy (Lessons 63–64)

- Every coach **has** a philosophy; it's in how they work and why they're in the field. Most never **put it into words**.
- An **official coaching philosophy** sets out the **core values and approaches** behind your unique way of coaching.
- It can be the **first step in building trust and rapport**, connecting potential clients to the right coach. "Nobody's everybody": not everyone will resonate with you, and that's fine.
- It may be **the bedrock of your brand and business**, because it is you. What makes you unique is what draws people in. New coaches tend to hold back what's unique to them, but clients are looking for **personal connection**, not just a list of skills.
- It is **not static**. It grows and changes as you do; revisit it.
- Writing it down builds **confidence**, draws in the people you want to serve and makes clear **what sets you apart**.
- You don't have to be "finished" or an expert in everything you coach. Your own wellness journey makes you a more empathic coach.

### The shared NASM foundation

| Core beliefs about clients | Foundational coaching tools |

| Trust the client's wisdom | Client-centred support |
| The client is the expert on their own lived experience | Leading through listening |
| Believe in the client's ability to change | Asking generative questions |
| See the client as capable and able | Active reflection |
| | Coaching from empathy and respect |

| | Merging expertise with inquiry |
| | Supporting self-efficacy |

### The five Ps

| P | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Your foundational **why** for entering coaching. It can come from your own experience, something you hope to share or help others avoid |
| **Principles** | What questions are at the heart of your work? What values do you amplify? |
| **Practices** | What methods, modalities and tools do you use, including your professional background and other skills? |
| **Pillars** | The NASM foundational components (above) |
| **People** | Who you want to serve |

### Reflection prompts (Lesson 64)

Lesson 64 offers sample questions a philosophy might be built around:

- What happens when people feel accepted and supported?
- Is balance actually the goal? If not, what is?
- How can we bring more emotional health practices into daily life?

## Coaching in practice

- "How would you like me to communicate with you, and how do you prefer to receive feedback?"
- "What role does your culture or family play in how you think about food, movement or rest?"
- "I may have made an assumption there. Can you tell me more about how it is for you?"
- If you misstep: "I'm sorry, I got that wrong. Thank you for telling me."
- For yourself: "Why did I become a coach? Who do I most want to serve? What do I believe happens when people feel supported?"
