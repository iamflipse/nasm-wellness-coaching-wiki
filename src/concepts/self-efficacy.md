# Self-Efficacy

*Lesson covered: 52 (Self-Efficacy Factors). Self-efficacy also runs through lessons 7, 10, 60, 69 and 71.*

## Key ideas

- **The end goal of coaching** is to build **self-efficacy**: the ability to become confident, self-aware and intentional about keeping up healthy habits **on your own**. Coaching is not about holding hands forever.
- Each client arrives with a **unique mix of efficacies**: the things they see themselves as good at. Someone may have high efficacy at work or in family life but low efficacy with nutrition and lifestyle.
- The lesson builds self-efficacy from four factors: **feedback**, **experiences**, **limiting factors** and **capacity**.

## The four factors

### 1. Feedback

Feedback is **not instructing**. Instructing gives evidence-based information. Feedback **holds up a mirror** so clients see themselves more clearly: their behaviour, outcomes and progress.

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Objective** | Based on data; presents measurable information |
| **Subjective** | Your opinion and what it's based on, followed by asking what the client thinks or feels about it |
| **Summative** | A periodic review of feedback after a period of working together |
| **Positive** | Recognising what is going well |
| **Critical** | Addresses what's needed to progress. Never "negative"; always make clear it's about **behaviour, not their value as a person** |

Feedback builds trust. As self-efficacy grows, even objective feedback starts to feel like **information rather than a personal failing**.

### 2. Experiences

Experiences are even more powerful than feedback.

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Mastery experiences** | The client builds on previous success and sees tangible evidence that success is possible. This grows their sense of skill |
| **Past experiences** | Shape how the client reads their current life and can open the way to new experiences |
| **Vicarious experiences** | Identifying with someone else's story, either an inspiring success or a cautionary tale |

The cycle: reflection, guidance and goal setting → new choices → process them with the coach → new mastery experiences → more self-efficacy.

### 3. Limiting factors

Everyone has some. Strategise to work **through or around** them. The course sorts them into three types:

- **Fixed**: cannot be changed
- **Malleable**: can be adjusted
- **Perceived**: may not really be a limitation at all

### 4. Capacity

The client's ability to **identify goals and motivations, map a route to them**, and engage in positive behaviours along the way. Capacity depends on **skill level, past experience and motivation** for change.

## Coaching in practice

- "Can I share what I've noticed over the last few weeks?" (summative feedback)
- "Here's what the numbers show. What do you make of that?" (objective feedback, then their interpretation)
- "This is about the routine, not about you as a person. Let's look at what got in the way."
- "Tell me about a time you succeeded at something like this before."
- "Is that something that truly can't change, or something we could work around?"
- "Is there someone whose story inspires you here?"
