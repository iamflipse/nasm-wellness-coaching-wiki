Coaching Mindset and Session Structure

Lessons covered: 50 (Cultivating a Coaching Mindset), 51 (Coaching Mindset Q&A), 66 (Coaching Tips), 67 (Coaching Mindset), 68 (Consistency in Session Structure)

What the coaching mindset is

The coaching mindset is a framework for how you engage in and approach your work. It is:

  • Client-centred, rooted in inquiry and empathy, in partnership and collaboration
  • Grounded in your self-awareness and commitment to the process
  • Based on trust: in the client's wisdom, experience, pace and capacity for change; the client's trust in you and in the space you create; and your trust in yourself and the process

You are not there to fix, direct or know everything. You are there to ask, listen, support, reflect and affirm (Lesson 67).

Holding clients capable and able: generative accountability

Traditional "accountability" can feel like nagging or obligation ("you did something wrong, you owe me"). The course reframes it:

  • The goals come from the client's own desires and vision, so buy-in and choice are built in.
  • The coach believes the client can do it, sometimes more than the client does. That models efficacy.
  • Generative accountability grows intrinsic motivation and self-efficacy: it is growthful, engaged and mutual.
  • When something doesn't happen, get curious: what worked, what didn't, what needs to shift? A "missed opportunity" becomes an opportunity for discovery.

Imposter feelings (Lessons 50 and 66)

  • New coaches often feel the imposter experience: doubting whether they are "enough".
  • You don't have to be perfect. You can serve from exactly where you are.
  • Imposter syndrome involves some shame and fear, and vulnerability helps: share your concern with people you trust and be open to feedback.
  • Let good feedback sink in. People in service roles often struggle to accept it.
  • The best cure is to get out there and use your skills to build someone else up.

The coach as mirror (Lesson 66)

  • The client does the work. The coach holds up a mirror and reflects their words back in new ways so they can see themselves from other angles.
  • We all have blind spots. Reflection is "the real meat of coaching", the part nobody can find by searching the internet.
  • Don't tell new clients what their transformation will look like. Telling can cloud what they think is possible and make the journey a straight-line "dart throw". Open a space to explore: clients often discover things they didn't know they wanted, or didn't want.
  • On movement: even when prescribing exercise, the goal is an independent practice outside sessions. Balance training time with coaching conversations, and keep goals and expectations clear.

The relationship is the vehicle (Lesson 51)

Remember how it feels when someone supports you wholeheartedly, listens and gives you space to reflect. Clients could get instructions anywhere. What is rare is someone who cares, believes in them and holds a vision for them through self-doubt. Build the relationship first; it is the vessel that carries your evidence-based advice.

Before and after a session (Lesson 67)

Coaching takes presence and generous attention. Get grounded, present, focused and relaxed.

Aim of every session: the client feels heard, respected and supported, with forward momentum on their wellness goals. You don't need all the answers.

Consistent session structure (Lesson 68)

  • Consistency builds trust and rapport. Offer a consistent coaching container so the client feels ease, confidence and dependability.
  • Standardise the components: how you open and close sessions and how you check in on goals and strategies.
  • The structure signals that this is intentional time, not a chat with a friend. It helps the client move from the chaos of their day into honesty and receptivity.
  • Consistent doesn't mean rigid. Stay personalised and responsive; adapt as you gain experience; ask for feedback on what is working.
  • Look after yourself as an essential resource: leave time for breaks.
  • Think long-term: goal timelines built on small, achievable wins that lead to bigger ones.
  • Respect the client's time.

Coaching in practice

  • "I know you really want to do this. How can I support you?"
  • "That didn't go as planned. What did you learn about what works for you?"
  • "What do you want to be true by the end of our time today?" (consistent opener)
  • "What are you taking away, and what's your next step before we meet again?" (consistent close)
  • "How is the way we work together going for you? Anything you'd change?"
  • Before a session, ask yourself: "What am I bringing in with me today, and what do I need to set down?"
