TermDefinitionLesson(s) / page

Allostatic loadThe cumulative wear and tear on the body from chronically activated stress responses32 · Stress
AmbivalenceHaving conflicting desires about change3, 10, 61 · OPSISE
Amplified reflectionRepeating a client's all-or-nothing statement back in exaggerated form so they can reconsider it73 · Derailers
Attribution / attributional retrainingWhat a person blames for blocking them (attribution), and retraining thoughts and perceptions to get past it with small, doable steps8 · Locus of Control
AutophagyDescribed in the course as the body's clean-up of metabolic waste: "carrying out the trash" at the cellular level38 · Dietary Patterns
BDNFA factor raised by exercise that builds new connections in the brain15, 21 · Movement Benefits
Beginner's mind with an expert toolkitBringing both competence and open curiosity; seeing each client as unique50, 63, 67 · Coaching Mindset
Body–food choice congruenceAwareness of how particular foods make your body feel (energy, fullness, nourishment)43 · Eating & Weight
CATChange-talk prompts: Commitment, Activation, Taking steps60 · MI
Change talkClient language that signals motivation to change60 · MI
Coaching containerThe safe, consistent space and structure in which coaching happens6, 68 · Coach's Role
Coaching communication cultureThe intentional way a coach relates to a client, combining the coach's style, training and the client's preferences62 · Cultural Awareness
Coaching from center, not as centerCoaching from a grounded place as a guide, not as the director of the client's journey50, 67 · Coaching Mindset
Coaching philosophyA written statement of the core values and approaches behind your way of coaching63, 64 · Philosophy
Cognitive loadThe demand on limited attention; when it rises, muscle tension (musculoskeletal load) rises too24 · Performance
Cognitive reappraisalReinterpreting an emotional event to lessen its impact; usually effective (unlike suppression)24 · Performance
DARNChange-talk prompts: Desire, Ability, Reasons, Need60 · MI
Dichotomous thinkingSeeing foods or behaviour as purely good or bad41 · Eating & Weight
Dietary patternThe totality of what a person habitually eats and drinks, with the parts acting together on health34, 36 · Dietary Patterns
Disinhibited eatingOvereating after feeling you've "broken a rule"41 · Eating & Weight
DistressStress perceived as overwhelming; harms performance, health and longevity15 · Movement Benefits
Dunbar's numberA suggested cognitive limit on the number of stable relationships a person can maintain30 · Social
Embodied cognitionThe theory that the whole body, not just the brain, takes part in thinking and producing behaviour26 · Performance
Emotional granularityThe ability to tell emotions apart precisely, allowing measured responses21 · Stress
EmpathyUnderstanding and sharing another person's feelings; not the same as sympathy49, 54 · Coach's Role
Equity awarenessRecognising that people don't start from the same place62 · Cultural Awareness
EustressPositive stress that drives growth and adaptation15, 23 · Stress
Explicit / implicit biasJudgements held consciously / stereotypes held without awareness62 · Cultural Awareness
External / internal locus of controlBelieving outcomes depend on others or chance / on your own actions7 · Locus of Control
Five PsPurpose, Principles, Practices, Pillars, People: a structure for a coaching philosophy63 · Philosophy
Fixed / growth mindsetBelieving abilities are set / believing they grow with effort and learning2, 24, 60 · Growth Mindset
Four FsFight, flight, food and sex: hypothalamus-driven survival motivations25 · Performance
Generative accountabilityAccountability reframed as holding clients capable and able: growthful, engaged and mutual, not nagging50, 54 · Coaching Mindset
Get to vs have toThe wellness mindset of choice and self-care ("I get to") rather than obligation and self-control ("I have to")70 · Get To vs Have To
Imposter syndromeDoubting your own skills and whether you have anything to offer50, 66 · Coaching Mindset
Intuitive eatingEating by physiological hunger and fullness cues rather than situational or emotional cues; 10 principles43 · Eating & Weight
Inverted UThe arousal–performance curve: too little or too much arousal hurts performance; the middle is the sweet spot24 · Performance
Locus of controlHow much control a person feels they have over their life and health7 · Locus of Control
Mastery experienceBuilding on previous success to grow belief in your own skill52 · Self-Efficacy
Mindful eatingPaying non-judgemental attention to hunger, fullness, taste, cravings and triggers while eating41, 43 · Eating & Weight
Motivational interviewing (MI)A collaborative, client-centred communication style that explores ambivalence and evokes the client's own motivation10, 56 · MI
Movement milestoneA meaningful physical accomplishment that is concrete evidence of growth53 · Movement Motivation
Negativity biasThe tendency to focus more on negative than positive things; an evolutionary survival trait9, 21, 30 · Negativity Bias
NeuroplasticityThe brain's ability to adapt to new circumstances and challenges, including memory and learning15, 22 · Movement Benefits
OARSOpen questions, Affirmations, Reflections, Summarising: the core MI tools10, 56 · MI
OPSISEThe six dimensions of wellness: Occupational, Physical, Social, Intellectual, Spiritual, Emotional3 · OPSISE
PERMA (+V)Seligman's wellbeing model (Positive emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, Accomplishment), with Vitality added19 · Positive Psychology
Positive psychologyThe scientific study of human wellbeing19 · Positive Psychology
Pragmatic optimismBeing honest about a hard situation, then choosing the most hopeful realistic next step20 · Positive Psychology
Psychological and behavioral flexibilityRecognising and adapting to situational demands while staying open and aware57 · Flexibility
Reflective listeningParaphrasing a client's words back to them to clarify and validate49, 56 · MI
Sedentary behaviourTime spent seated, reclining or lying down; exercise does not undo it14 · Movement Benefits
Self-compassionCommon humanity, self-kindness and mindfulness towards your own struggles; not self-indulgence9, 55 · Self-Compassion
Self-determination theoryThe theory, linked to MI, that people inherently seek growth; autonomy and competence fuel change10 · MI
Self-efficacyConfidence and intentionality about sustaining healthy habits on your own; the end goal of coaching52 · Self-Efficacy
ShameThe painful belief that we are flawed and so unworthy of love and belonging54 · Shame
Sleep inertiaGrogginess from waking during deep sleep; avoided with 15-minute or 90-minute naps27 · Sleep
Social capitalQuality relationships and communities built on trust and belonging31 · Social
Subjective vitalityThe perceived feeling of having enough energy15 · Movement Benefits
SummarisingThe "S" in OARS: pulling the whole conversation together10, 56 · MI
Toxic positivityDenying real suffering and insisting on focusing only on the good20 · Positive Psychology
Transfer effectA positive change in one area spreading to others58, 59 · Upward Spirals
Upward spiralA positive feedback loop where one positive behaviour or emotion makes the next more likely58 · Upward Spirals
Vagal toneDescribed in the course as a measure of stress, linked to the 10th cranial nerve; it forms an upward spiral with positive emotion22, 58 · Stress
Vicarious experienceBeing influenced by identifying with someone else's success or cautionary tale52 · Self-Efficacy
Von Economo neuronsBrain cells found only in highly intelligent, social animals, linked to regulating emotion in social contexts30 · Social
Weight-neutral approachFocusing on health-promoting behaviours rather than weight loss as the outcome42 · Eating & Weight
Wellness coaching (NASM)The active process of applying learned and developed strategies that lead to the mental, emotional and physical betterment of life3 · OPSISE
Wellness visionThe client's personal picture of what wellness means and is in service of; it sits at the top of the wellness plan69, 70 · Wellness Plan
