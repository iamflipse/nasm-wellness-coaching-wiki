Psychological and Behavioral Flexibility

Lesson covered: 57 (Psychological and Behavioral Flexibility)

Key ideas

  • Living in the moment means focusing on the now, in body and mind, without the mind wandering to to-do lists or the next meal.
  • Psychological and behavioral flexibility is the ability to recognise and adapt to what a situation demands while staying open and aware. The lesson calls it the "superpower" that lets you live in the now.
  • It brings greater self-awareness and the ability to pause and notice what is happening.
  • Your presence is contagious: a coach's ability to stay present affects the client's ability to stay present.
  • Bringing the client back to the meaning behind a goal helps them remember their why.

What gets in the way

ObstacleWhat helps
Holding patterns (being stuck)Understand where the pattern comes from
Overriding emotions that talk the client out of a planned behaviourAcknowledgement and empathy so they feel heard and validated
Changing values and beliefs, which may drift from the original wellness visionListen with compassion; reflect, affirm and collaborate
Distress signals: anxiety, avoidance, the urge to escapeTolerance grows as they are worked through, and adaptability strengthens
Negative self-talk, especially when motivation is lowExplore where it comes from (societal, cultural, family) and build tools to counter it

Building flexibility

  • Use established goals and values as a compass to keep momentum heading towards the vision.
  • Let clients express themselves. That in itself builds flexibility.
  • Create space for change by brainstorming and preparing for obstacles in advance.
  • Encourage self-compassion to improve wellbeing and prevent burnout.
  • Normalise falling off track. If clients expect setbacks, they can accept them in the moment and move on. Setbacks can be reframed as stepping stones.
  • Rehearse scenarios. Talk through what could delay progress and let the client role-play roadblocks in a safe, non-judgemental space. This weakens limiting beliefs and fears.
  • Shift perspective to a positive view that can be turned into action.
  • Always guide the client back to what motivates them and use it as the launch point.

Behaviour change is not a straight line. Expect ups and downs. The coach's role is to help the client get back on track into goal-aligned behaviour.

Coaching in practice

  • "Let's come back to why this goal matters to you."
  • "What might get in the way this week? What would you do if it did?"
  • "Want to play it out? I'll be the colleague offering cake at 3 p.m."
  • "Slipping up is part of this. What's one thing you'd want to remember when it happens?"
  • "Where do you think that voice saying 'you always quit' comes from?"
  • "Has anything shifted in what matters to you since we set this goal?"
