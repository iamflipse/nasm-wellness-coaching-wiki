Supplements and Flexible Dietary Strategies

Lessons covered: 44 (Supplementation), 45 (Supplementation Q&A), 46 (Flexible Dietary Strategies)

Why supplements can play a role (Lesson 44)

The expert gives several reasons someone might need more of a nutrient than their diet gives them:

ReasonExample
Common dietary gapsA skewed diet that leaves out whole food groups
Individual choicesAvoiding certain foods
Genetic or epigenetic differencesSome people have a higher need for a particular nutrient
Training loadHeavy training can raise needs, for example for electrolytes

Supplements can fill gaps. They do not replace a healthy dietary pattern (see Healthy Dietary Patterns).

Magnesium example

  • Magnesium sits at the centre of chlorophyll, so dark leafy greens are a major source. People who don't eat greens may fall short.
  • The expert links low magnesium status to metabolic syndrome, prediabetes and diabetes, and to a tendency towards high blood pressure, weight gain and higher blood sugar.

Spotting likely gaps (Lesson 45, Q&A)

The Q&A is partly cut off in the transcript. The usable points:

  • Nuts and seeds are rich in minerals such as zinc and magnesium. Dark leafy greens are a key magnesium source.
  • Iron example: someone who says they're tired, never eats red meat and trains two hours a day is quite likely iron-deficient, and could add iron-rich foods.

Look at what the client does and doesn't eat and how they train to spot likely gaps. The expert's suggested fix in the example is food (iron-rich foods).

Flexible dietary strategies (Lesson 46)

  • Diet is one of the hardest areas because habits and diet composition vary so much between people.
  • The real goal is to teach variety.
  • Eat more vegetables. The expert cites the US National Cancer Institute recommending 9–13 servings of vegetables a day to reduce cancer risk.
  • Lead with empathy: understand and respect where the client is coming from, then guide and reshape their decisions towards the outcome they want.

Coaching in practice

  • "Are there any food groups you don't eat, by choice or because of how they make you feel?"
  • "How much are you training at the moment? How's your energy?"
  • "How often do leafy greens, nuts or seeds make it onto your plate?"
  • "What's one vegetable you could add this week?"
  • "Would you be open to hearing what the research says about this nutrient?" (ask permission before sharing)
  • Stay within your scope of practice (Lesson 73) when a deficiency is suspected.
