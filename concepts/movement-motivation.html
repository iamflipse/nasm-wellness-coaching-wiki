Movement Motivation, Meaning and Obstacles

Lessons covered: 11 (Movement Why), 16 (Client-Centered Expectations), 17 (Overcoming Obstacles), 18 (Incorporating Movement), 53 (Meaning in Movement, titled "Get to vs Have to")

Finding the "why" (Lesson 11)

Most people have more than one motivation, and motivations change over time. The coach's job is to steer clients towards the motivations most likely to build a positive, long-term relationship with movement.

MotivationEffect on sticking with movement
Weight loss or appearance (as the sole focus)Tends to undermine finding enjoyable, lasting movement
External pressure ("you need to do this workout")Unhelpful
Internal pressure ("you used to train three times a week, now look at you")Unhelpful
Health, energy, coping with stress, moodGood starting motivations
Enjoyment of the activityStrong long-term driver
Personal challenge: doing something hard and meaningful, getting better, feeling proudStrong long-term driver

Connect movement to the big why

  • Link activity to the client's wellness vision, which may not be a fitness goal: more energy, managing stress, rebuilding after illness, purpose after a breakup or job loss.
  • Share immediate payoffs:
    • Feel-better effect: a few minutes of anything that raises heart rate or breathing or uses muscles brings more energy, less stress and more positive emotion.
    • Exercise high: around 20 minutes or more changes brain chemistry, mood and outlook, and even helps you connect with others.
    • Benefits to every body system, especially when going from very little to getting started.
    • The upward spiral: on active days people make more progress on important goals, have more positive interactions, and are less affected by stress.
  • Frame movement as a chance to do something you enjoy: nature, music, people, time alone, play, competition, learning.
  • Remember your why before, during (especially when it's hard) and after (savouring). Thinking about the why when planning increases commitment. Positive expectations increase the pleasure and meaning people actually get from the session.

Exercise for coaches: the sentence completion "I ___ because ___" (for example "I run because…"), answered as many times as you can. Then ask which answers get to the heart of it.

Meaning in movement (Lesson 53)

Movement creates feelings, and those feelings say something about who you are and what you can do ("I am strong", "I am free", "I'm connected to something bigger"). The expert describes how kickboxing makes them feel like a fighter, which matters to someone living with anxiety and past depression.

Three ways to find meaning in movement:

PathCoach's approach
1. How it makes you feelHelp clients find an activity that makes them feel how they want to feel: powerful, confident, graceful, joyful, present, competitive. Lead discovery, not prescription. Encourage trying things
2. Movement milestonesA physical accomplishment you couldn't do, or didn't think you could. Milestones are concrete evidence of growth and can rewrite limiting beliefs. Imagining a future one creates a "future self-defining memory"
3. Movement communityBelonging, shared goals (a strength gym, a community garden), collective experiences

Follow the client's energy and curiosity. Be a mirror that magnifies the meaning of movement back to them.

Client-centred expectations (Lesson 16)

  • Ask permission before giving more information.
  • With clients who have low efficacy or capacity, examine stumbling blocks together and turn them into stepping stones.
  • The coach's expectation is to hold clients accountable to their own potential and be a mirror for their growth.

Overcoming obstacles (Lesson 17)

ObstacleCoaching response
Lack of timeClients decide how they spend their time. Suggest a time log, scheduling activity, walking meetings, movement breaks; use MI to explore discrepancies
Goal conflictTwo valued goals compete (more family time vs more exercise). Help identify and clarify
Role conflictA self-image (for example "always in control") makes trying something new feel risky. The client may not be aware of it
Limiting social beliefsFear of being judged, rooted in fear of not belonging
Activity vs recoveryToo much activity at the expense of rest leads to burnout
Past negative experiencesFear and pain points. Above all, supportive active listening
Psychological challenges (anxiety, depression, grief, trauma)Remind them of the evidence linking movement and wellbeing; focus on what they can do right now

You are not responsible for rebooting a client's life overnight. Clients change as they find the capacity, opportunity and motivation. Their obstacles are never trivial.

Incorporating movement (Lesson 18)

A good experience creates a positive feedback loop: the client expects that investing a bit more (a walk, a class) will pay off even more. Purpose and physical activity reinforce each other (see Upward Spirals).

Coaching in practice

  • "What do you want to feel when you move?"
  • "What's something physical you'd be proud to be able to do, something you'd want on video?"
  • "Do you like the idea of having a movement community?"
  • "When you think about being more active, what is it really in service of?"
  • "Do I like who I am here? Is this helping me become who I want to be?" (questions the expert suggests clients ask themselves)
  • "What does your week actually look like? Where could a 10-minute walk fit?"
